# All Things Retro
I create easy-to-follow guides for setting up retro gaming systems, covering everything from choosing the right hardware and controllers to installing emulators, configuring displays, and organising game libraries. My aim is to help beginners and enthusiasts build reliable, enjoyable retro gaming setups with clear instructions and practical tips. In this guide I will be setting this up on my AYN Thor which I absolutely love and has got be back into retro games I can play when lounging on the sofa. 

## Recommended Accessories (optional)

- MicroSD Card Reader
- HDMI Out (If you want to extend to an external display)

## Where Can I get ROM's/Games?
You can get ROMs legally from:
- Official re-releases: Nintendo Switch Online, Virtual Console, Sega Genesis Classics, Atari 50, Capcom Arcade Stadium, and similar collections.
- Homebrew and freeware libraries: PDROMS and RomHacking.net host many independently made or freely distributed games.
- Internet Archive: It has public-domain, freeware, shareware, and preservation material, but not every uploaded ROM is legally distributable—check the item’s rights/license before downloading.
- Your own cartridges or discs: Dump ROMs/ISOs from games you own using appropriate hardware. Whether personal backups are legal varies by country, and downloading someone else’s dump usually isn’t automatically legal just because you own the original.
Commercial retro services: Some publishers sell classic games through Steam, GOG, console stores, or dedicated collections; these generally provide the game in an authorized format rather than as a standalone ROM.
> [!NOTE]  
> Avoid large “full ROM set” downloads unless every included game is clearly public domain, freeware, or specifically licensed. Collection labels alone do not establish legal distribution rights.

## What about BIOS's?
It can be legal to obtain a BIOS, but it depends on where it comes from and your jurisdiction:
- Usually legal: Dumping the BIOS from hardware you own for your personal use, where local backup laws allow it.
- Usually legal: Downloading an open-source or replacement BIOS released under a license permitting redistribution.
Usually not legal: Downloading a proprietary console BIOS from an unofficial ROM/BIOS site, even if you own the console. Ownership of the hardware generally doesn’t grant permission to download someone else’s copy.
- Clearly legal: Obtaining it through an official publisher or authorized emulator package.
- For the safest route, use a compatible BIOS-dumping tool with your own console, or use a documented open-source replacement BIOS. Don’t assume that a site’s claim that a download is “legal” makes it so; copyright and anti-circumvention rules vary by country.

## First things out the box
Upon opening the box to your shiny new device.
1. Connect to your home internet
2. Then head to **Settings** > **System** > **Thor OTA**
3. Check for updates
4. It may require a device restart depending on the firmware (always recommended restart after every large update)

*The current AYN Thor version of me writing this guide is version: 1.0.0.377*
